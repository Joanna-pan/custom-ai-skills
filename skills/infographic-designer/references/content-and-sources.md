# Content And Sources

## Supplied Content

- Preserve the user's intended meaning and terminology.
- Distinguish copyediting from substantive rewriting.
- For complex work, show material cuts, merged points, or changed claims before production.
- Never silently "improve" a number, date, quotation, legal phrase, or technical condition.

## Research Mode

When only a topic is supplied:

1. Define the factual questions needed by the infographic.
2. Prefer primary and authoritative sources; use current sources for unstable facts.
3. Record the exact claim, source URL, publisher, publication/update date, and access date.
4. Reconcile conflicting figures or disclose the disagreement.
5. Keep units, time periods, populations, and denominators attached to numbers.
6. Separate evidence from interpretation and correlation from causation.

Do not fabricate a source to complete a layout. If evidence is missing, revise or remove the claim.

## Source Presentation

For researched or complex factual infographics, include compact source markers in the visual when appropriate, a source slide/page or sidecar source list with full links, and a structured long description containing the essential information and relationships.

Complex graphics need both a short identifier and a fuller textual alternative. See W3C guidance: https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/

## Privacy And External Tools

- Treat unpublished business processes, client data, personal data, and internal metrics as confidential by default.
- Do not send confidential material to an external image, embedding, or research service without explicit permission.
- Redact or replace sensitive values before generating decorative assets when the asset does not need real data.

## Final Content Checks

- Every number has a unit and scope.
- Every acronym is expanded or appropriate for the audience.
- Labels use consistent terms and grammatical forms.
- Dates and versions are explicit where relevant.
- Sources support the exact visible claims.
- The title states the subject; the subtitle or takeaway explains why it matters.
